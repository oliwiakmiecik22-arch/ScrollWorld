# Your Cocktail Guide — Art Direction

## North star

**A forbidden field guide found behind a velvet curtain at 2 a.m.**

The visual world combines hand-inked underground comics, mid-century liquor advertising, theatrical scenery, and psychedelic nightlife print. It should feel lush, tactile, expressive, colorful, and slightly improper—never polished into corporate luxury. Ink spreads, colors miss their edges, reflections bend incorrectly, and shadows sometimes behave like characters.

## Original visual signature

The system has four recognizable parts:

1. **Hero glass silhouette:** one unmistakable vessel dominates each chapter.
2. **The Golden Twist:** one curling guide changes material but keeps its gesture.
3. **Printed depth:** flat illustrated planes create immersion through parallax, masks, and selective refraction.
4. **Emotional color takeover:** each desire owns a palette, then donates one color or material to the next scene.

This combination is the project identity. Do not reproduce reference-site characters, layouts, logos, assets, or distinctive visual jokes.

## Style recipe

- **45% retro editorial illustration:** economical shapes, assertive silhouettes, limited palette per scene.
- **25% underground comic:** expressive line weight, awkward elegance, and visual wit.
- **20% cinematic material study:** condensation, refraction, brine, crema, bubbles, ice, smoke, and liquid edges.
- **10% psychedelic rupture:** impossible changes of scale or matter at carefully chosen transitions.

Avoid airbrushed fantasy art, photoreal bottle advertising, clean isometric dioramas, glossy 3D lettering, vaporwave clichés, tiki caricature, and sterile product cinematography.

## Modular world kit

Every cocktail world is assembled from the same asset slots:

1. **Atmosphere backplate:** one wide illustrated environment.
2. **Rear depth plane:** architecture, horizon, cloud, or light geometry.
3. **Hero media slot:** Magnific video plus exact poster frame.
4. **Foreground occluder:** curtain, foliage, chrome, shadow, or table edge.
5. **Golden Twist path:** one chapter-specific material treatment.
6. **History ephemera set:** maximum three objects.
7. **Ingredient symbols:** one icon/shape per ingredient, capped at six.
8. **Texture and particle layer:** selected from a shared kit.

This cap prevents ten chapters from becoming ten bespoke illustration projects. Difference comes from art direction and composition, not layer count.

## Illustration language

### Line and shape

- Thick exterior ink contours and finer interior material marks.
- Curves dominate glass, peel, smoke, garnish, and human silhouettes.
- Hard geometry belongs to architecture, labels, chrome, and city light.
- Small registration errors between line and fill are encouraged.
- Each scene uses three to five depth planes, not dozens of fragments.
- Glass geometry remains correct enough for bartenders to recognize the vessel immediately.

### Texture

- Coarse halftone in large shadows.
- Fine paper tooth in flat color.
- Dry-brush edges in smoke, mint, cloud, and heat haze.
- Screenprint misregistration on headlines and flash accents.
- Subtle grain inside liquid gradients.
- Sparse sharp highlights on wet glass and chrome.

Texture must be scale-aware. A universal noise overlay would flatten every material into the same surface.

### Glass and liquid

- Describe glass through highlights, refraction, and displaced scenery rather than a gray outline.
- Use no more than two dominant specular streaks per vessel.
- Keep liquid edges saturated and slightly imperfect.
- Condensation varies in size, merges occasionally, and responds to gravity.
- Ice uses a few bold fractures rather than photoreal micro-detail.
- Hero videos may amplify one material behavior only; they should not pour, smoke, sparkle, rotate, and explode at once.

## The Golden Twist

The motif remains recognizable through movement and curvature rather than one literal material.

### Shape rules

- One continuous tapered stroke with a broader middle.
- Default gesture: long S-curve followed by a small reverse curl.
- May pass in front of and behind the glass to reinforce depth.
- Appears peel-gold at the entrance or exit of important transitions even when it adopts local color.
- Never grows a face, hands, or mascot behavior.

### Behavioral rules

- Enters before guidance is needed.
- Touches the next focal object once.
- Does not loop as meaningless ambient decoration.
- At rest becomes plausible material: peel, reflection, contrail, steam, salt edge, pick, bubble stream, or mint stem.

## Dirty Martini focus direction

Dirty Martini must be treated as a peak of intimacy, not an interlude between whiskey and pink glamour.

- **Provocative:** the olive is oversized and enters as a shadow before the real object.
- **Sophisticated:** the composition is sparse, exact, and cold; no comic slapstick.
- **Salty:** brine appears as cloudy mineral movement, not murky dirt.
- **Intimate:** the camera stays close, like a private booth seen through condensation.
- **Slightly forbidden:** a chrome pick cuts black velvet; the history graphic lets green spread beyond a clean Martini diagram.
- **Nocturnal:** black absorbs most of the frame; only olive, chrome, and glass receive light.
- **Surreal distortion:** background reflections bend behind cloudy liquid as though the room breathes. Distortion remains slow and subtle.

The black velvet must stay tactile and matte. Chrome should be a thin cold accent, not a futuristic interface. The olive needs real skin texture, wetness, and weight without becoming photoreal food advertising.

## Typography

### Proposed family system

- **Display:** Fraunces Black / Black Italic for voluptuous headlines and cocktail names.
- **Utility and recipe:** Archivo Narrow Medium / Semibold for labels, quantities, and methods.
- **Annotation accent:** a limited custom hand-lettered set for dates, arrows, doubts, and marginal notes.

Validate licenses and language coverage before production.

### Type behavior

- Cocktail names may sit behind glass, but a readable foreground label must also exist.
- History text uses a measure of roughly 45–60 characters.
- Recipe quantities align even when their symbols move spatially.
- Use uppercase for labels, not entire paragraphs.
- Italics provide sensuality; condensed capitals provide bartender precision.
- Never render final text into Magnific footage.

### Modular variation

All chapters use one typographic scale. Variation is limited to title angle, alignment, and one emotional behavior:

- Negroni overlaps.
- Daiquiri opens wide.
- Aviation floats.
- Espresso doubles as an afterimage.
- Margarita breaks the margin.
- Old Fashioned locks to a firm baseline.
- Dirty Martini sits close and low.
- Cosmopolitan catches a flash duplicate.
- French 75 rises vertically.
- Mai Tai stacks exuberantly.

## Composition

### Desktop

- Hero glass occupies 40–58% of viewport height.
- Preserve the central 35% of frame width as the hero-video safe corridor.
- Alternate copy left and right according to the Golden Twist’s travel.
- Use asymmetry and overlap while keeping text on a quiet underlying column system.
- Foreground planes may crop aggressively to place the viewer inside the scene.

### Mobile

- Hero glass occupies 34–46% of viewport height.
- The 16:9 video uses a controlled center crop; essential garnish stays inside the safe corridor.
- Ingredient labels collapse into one readable vertical sequence.
- Use only foreground, hero, and background depth planes.
- Wide scenery remains atmospheric and expendable; never shrink the hero to preserve it.

## Magnific hero-video art direction

All ten hero videos follow one visual contract:

- 4–6 seconds, desktop-first 16:9, center-safe.
- Slow push, pedestal, or restrained lateral drift; never handheld.
- Stable opening and closing frames with the same glass topology.
- One main material action and at most two secondary cues.
- Illustrated screenprint/ink finish integrated into the footage.
- No visible text, logos, branded bottles, hands, faces, or accidental extra garnish.
- No sterile black-background luxury spin, full 360-degree orbit, explosive pour, or photoreal studio pack shot.
- Motion must be reversible enough for scroll scrubbing.

Each video should feel like a living illustration, not footage placed inside an illustrated page.

## Motion direction

Motion uses three speeds:

1. **Breath:** condensation, bubbles, smoke, mint, light drift.
2. **Gesture:** peel curls, labels enter, ingredients assemble, the Golden Twist points.
3. **Rupture:** four major world transformations only.

Most of the journey stays in Breath. Gesture clarifies content. Rupture is rare and followed by stillness. Ten equally loud chapters would become numbing.

### Easing

- Glass and liquid: weighted slow-in/slow-out.
- Peel, steam, and mint: elastic curves with almost no bounce.
- Labels and ephemera: brisk mechanical slides, folds, or stamps.
- Code transitions: one clear direction and one acceleration point.
- Major videos: controlled camera pull with an obvious material match.

## WebGL restraint

Use at most one persistent/shared Three.js canvas and a small family of reusable effects:

- Refractive portal/displacement.
- Particle migration between two materials.
- Gentle liquid-color takeover.
- Depth dust or bubbles.

Do not model ten glasses in 3D. Do not use WebGL for typography, core recipes, navigation, or any information required for access. Every shader effect needs a CSS/GSAP poster fallback.

## History and recipe visual systems

### History

Each origin beat is built from a maximum of three ephemera objects selected from a shared grammar: receipt, map, stamp, matchbook, ticket, newspaper strip, diagram, or bar check. This keeps production finite while allowing era-specific storytelling.

### Recipe

Use three reusable presentation patterns:

1. **Orbit:** three or four ingredients circle the glass.
2. **Assembly:** symbols enter a shaker or mixing-glass outline in order.
3. **Strata:** five or six ingredients stack, then collapse into the build.

Methods remain readable text next to the visual action. Decorative symbols never replace ingredient names or quantities.

## Human presence

People remain peripheral atmosphere: a gloved hand cropped at the edge, a profile behind wet glass, dancers reduced to two-color silhouettes. There is no central human protagonist and no recurring character. Vary silhouettes without turning identity into decoration or stereotype.

## Image-generation and finishing guidance

When production begins, approve one global style frame and the Dirty Martini contrast frame before generating the full set. Every prompt should carry the same anchors: hand-inked retro editorial illustration, imperfect screenprint layers, cinematic glass material, nocturnal surrealism, and center-safe composition.

Generated output is a starting plate. Finish by:

- Correcting glassware, garnish, and ingredient errors.
- Removing accidental logos, bottle marks, extra objects, and pseudo-text.
- Matching line weight, grain, black level, and highlight language across chapters.
- Separating static foreground, history, and atmosphere layers where needed.
- Checking citrus, olive, mint, cherries, coffee beans, ice, and measures for plausibility.

Do not use third-party artwork as a direct style target. Describe qualities instead of naming or reproducing a living artist’s exact style.

## Accessibility and sensory balance

- Maintain readable contrast independently from video color.
- Avoid rapid flashes; Cosmopolitan uses two soft blooms, not strobing.
- Avoid constant rotation and high-density particles.
- Reduced motion replaces video and travel with stable posters and short dissolves.
- Audio, if later added, remains optional, muted by default, and irrelevant to comprehension.

## Rejection checklist

Reject a frame if it:

- Looks like a sterile premium-spirit commercial or photoreal pack shot.
- Uses a centered headline-and-button landing-page formula.
- Turns recipes into white cards or a dashboard grid.
- Makes scenery more important than the cocktail.
- Relies on sexualized bodies instead of material atmosphere.
- Makes Daiquiri or Mai Tai depend on generic tiki stereotypes.
- Makes the four V-shaped/coupe cocktails look interchangeable.
- Makes Dirty Martini bright, clean, comic, or daytime.
- Uses more than one loud effect at once.
- Copies a recognizable character, composition, or asset from a reference.
